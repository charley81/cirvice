# Preliminary product-name research: “Cirvice”

**Research date:** November 12, 2026  
**Research ticket:** [Wayfinder issue #3](https://github.com/charley81/cirvice/issues/3)  
**Question:** Whether “Cirvice” is a practical name for the planned U.S. neighborhood-services marketplace described in [Wayfinder map issue #1](https://github.com/charley81/cirvice/issues/1).  
**Decision:** **Do not adopt “Cirvice” as the final public name without a qualified U.S. trademark attorney’s clearance search and opinion. Prefer choosing a different name now.**

This is a preliminary, non-legal screening—not trademark clearance. The favorable facts are that no exact **CIRVICE** federal mark or exact-name app was found and several important domains appear unregistered. Those advantages are outweighed by (1) live, phonetically close marks in software and online-service fields, especially **CERVIS** and **SIRVIS**; (2) the likely pronunciation of “Cirvice” as the ordinary word “service,” which creates spelling, search, distinctiveness, and word-of-mouth friction; and (3) an already fragmented set of exact social handles.

## Product context and search method

The proposed product is a private, mobile-first web marketplace where approved residents discover and publish neighborhood services and exchange inquiries. It is not intended to process bookings or payments at launch.[^map]

The screening covered:

- the USPTO Trademark Search system for the exact term and close visual/phonetic variants, including **CIRVIS, CERVIS, SIRVIS, SIRVICE, CIRVISE, CERVISE, SERVIS, SIRVO,** and related `CIRV*`/`SERVIS*` results, with particular attention to International Classes 9, 35, and 42;
- USPTO TSDR records for the most relevant live and dead results;
- first-party sites for close software/service brands;
- the official North Carolina and South Carolina business-search portals;
- registry RDAP, DNS, and WHOIS observations for exact domains;
- Apple’s U.S. App Store search API, Google Play, and major platform profile/search pages; and
- exact-name web searches for apparent common-law use.

The USPTO warns that marks need not be identical: similarity in sound, appearance, meaning, or commercial impression can matter, together with whether the goods or services are related.[^loc] It also says a comprehensive search should extend beyond federal records to state registries, domains, and internet/common-law use.[^clearance]

### Preliminary confusion assessment

The federal search produces enough risk that an exact-name no-match should not be treated as a green light:

1. **Sound is the central weakness.** In ordinary U.S. English, “Cirvice” is likely to be spoken as “service” or “sir-viss.” That puts it close to both **CERVIS** and **SIRVIS**, and users hearing the name may type `service`, `cervis`, or `sirvis` instead.
2. **The software channel overlaps.** Cirvice will be a web software service. **CERVIS** is live SaaS, while **SIRVIS**, **CIRVIA**, and the pending **SERVIST ENERGY** filing also occupy technology/service territory.
3. **The CERVIS use is not abstract.** Its current product coordinates people, sign-ups, groups, and communications. That is not the same product, but it is close enough in channel and community-facing workflow to require professional analysis.
4. **“Service” is the product’s subject matter.** The intentional misspelling may communicate the category, but it can also make the name feel descriptive, typo-like, and harder to own or search. No conclusion about registrability is made here.

## Company names, state records, and common-law use

No obvious U.S. operating company or product using the exact spelling **Cirvice** surfaced in the reviewed exact-name web and major-platform results. The prominent exact results were this project, occupied personal/social profiles, surname/pronunciation pages, and apparent misspellings of “service.” That suggests a light exact-name web footprint, not absence of common-law rights.

A verified no-match could **not** be obtained from the two launch-state business registries in this environment:

- the [South Carolina Secretary of State Business Name Search](https://businessfilings.sc.gov/BusinessFiling/Entity/Search) loaded, but submission required a reCAPTCHA response;
- the [North Carolina Secretary of State Business Registration Search](https://www.sosnc.gov/online_services/search/by_title/_Business_Registration) presented automated-access security verification. North Carolina separately states that its business-registration data is updated weekly and available as CSV through its data-subscription service.[^nc-data]

Accordingly, this research makes **no claim that “Cirvice” is available as an entity, assumed, or reserved name in either state**. A person should manually search exact and similar names in both official portals immediately before formation. Counsel should also check state trademark/service-mark records; the USPTO’s state-resource page points applicants to the individual North Carolina and South Carolina offices.[^state-links]

Entity-name availability would not itself answer the trademark question: two businesses can sometimes coexist under state naming rules while trademark use still creates confusion.

**Practical conclusion:** an exact `.com` appears obtainable, which is a real advantage. It does not reduce trademark risk, and no domain has been reserved by this research. Availability can change at any moment. If counsel approves the name, acquire the core domains before public disclosure.

## App stores

- Apple’s official U.S. Search API returned 97 fuzzy/relevance results for `cirvice`, but **none had an exact `trackName` of “Cirvice.”**[^apple-query] Apple documents that Search API results are generated from the submitted search term and selected media/entity parameters.[^apple-docs]
- The [Google Play U.S. app search for `cirvice`](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=cirvice&c=apps&hl=en_US&gl=US) returned service-related fuzzy matches, with no exact **Cirvice** app name observed.

This is favorable for future app-title practicality, but Cirvice is planned as a web application at launch, store results are dynamic, and app-store naming rules are separate from trademark rights.

## Recommendation

**Treat “Cirvice” as unsuitable for final adoption at this stage.** The domain position and sparse exact-name footprint are attractive, but they do not compensate for the live **CERVIS** SaaS registration/use, live **SIRVIS** technology-services registration/use, likely “service” pronunciation, and occupied handles.

The lowest-cost path is to generate alternatives that:

- do not sound like the generic category word “service”;
- are farther from live Class 9, 35, and 42 marks;
- have a clean `.com` or credible modifier domain; and
- can support a substantially consistent handle set.

If the team still wants **Cirvice**, do not launch or invest materially in the brand until a qualified U.S.-licensed trademark attorney:

1. runs a comprehensive federal search using phonetic, spelling, translation, prefix/suffix, and coordinated-class strategies;
2. analyzes **CERVIS**, **SIRVIS**, **SIRVO**, **CIRVIA**, **SERVIST ENERGY**, and any later-filed records against the final identification of goods/services;
3. searches all relevant state trademark and business-name records, including manual SC and NC checks;
4. commissions or performs a broader U.S. common-law search covering company databases, trade publications, local businesses, products, domains, and marketplace listings;
5. checks ownership, actual use, enforcement history, assignments, and pending proceedings for the closest marks; and
6. advises on registrability, infringement risk, filing strategy, and whether any coexistence or consent analysis is appropriate.

## Limits of this research

- This document is informational research, not legal advice or a legal opinion.
- It does **not** claim trademark clearance, registrability, non-infringement, or name availability.
- Search results are a time-limited snapshot and can change immediately.
- The search was not a paid comprehensive common-law search, a complete 50-state search, or an international clearance search.
- SC and NC official business-name searches were not completed because their public portals required interactive anti-bot verification.
- A dead federal registration can coexist with continuing common-law use; a live registration can also have facts not apparent from the summary record.
- Search engines, app stores, and social platforms are incomplete and personalized, and a missing result does not prove non-use.
- The final risk depends on the exact mark presentation, pronunciation, launch geography, goods/services wording, channels, purchasers, and evidence of actual marketplace use.
